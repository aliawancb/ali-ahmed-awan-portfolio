Your strategy is only as good as your execution

Moving a lifestyle app from free trial to freemium was the easy part to decide. Forty customer interviews, a rebuilt onboarding flow, and a Saudi launch were what actually turned a good call into 13% to 26% paid conversion.

Ali Ahmed Awan · May 28, 2024 · 7 min read

At The ENTERTAINER, the strategic decision was almost boringly obvious once we looked at the data: free trial with one-off purchases was training users to churn right after the thing they wanted. Freemium, with a real subscription underneath, was the correct call. I could have written that recommendation in a single slide, and in a sense, I did. The slide wasn't the hard part. The eighteen months after it were.

Where strategies actually die

"Move to freemium" is a sentence. Underneath it were decisions nobody puts in the strategy deck: which features stay free forever versus which become the reason to upgrade, how to not punish the loyal customers who'd already paid under the old model, how to rebuild an onboarding flow so the free tier still demonstrated enough value to make upgrading obvious rather than extortionate. Get any one of those wrong and "go freemium" becomes a strategy that was correct on paper and a failure in the App Store reviews.

We ran over 40 customer interviews specifically to find where the free tier would feel like a trap instead of a taste. That's not a strategy activity, strategy had already been decided. It's execution, and it's where the actual conversion lift came from.

Nobody has ever been fired for a correct one-slide strategy that nobody could execute. They should be. The slide was never the valuable part.

The numbers that prove the point

In practice

Paid conversion moved from 13% to 26%, and retention from 33% to 42%, not at the moment we approved the freemium strategy, but across the months of onboarding rebuilds, pricing-tier testing, and the Saudi launch that followed it. The strategy call explains the direction. The execution explains the size of the number.

What good execution actually looks like, concretely

  • It's unglamorous. Most of the work was rewriting empty-state screens and fixing a paywall that triggered at the wrong moment, not big swings.
  • It's willing to contradict the strategy's first draft. Interviews told us the initial free-tier limits were too tight; we loosened them before launch, which felt like watering down the strategy and was actually what let it work.
  • It has a owner who stays past the decision. The person who argued for freemium in the strategy meeting needs to still be in the room eight months later when the onboarding flow is on its fourth rewrite.

The test I use now

Before I sign off on any strategic call, I ask what the ugliest six months of executing it will look like, specifically, not generally. If nobody in the room can describe that in detail, the strategy isn't ready to be called a decision yet. It's still an opinion with a nice slide.
