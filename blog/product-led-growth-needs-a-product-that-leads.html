Product-led growth only works if the product actually leads

Everyone wants the label. Fewer teams want the onboarding rebuild, the removed sales call, and the uncomfortable month where self-serve revenue looks worse before it looks better.

Ali Ahmed Awan · October 5, 2023 · 6 min read

"We're going product-led" is one of the easiest sentences to say in a strategy meeting and one of the hardest to actually do. I've watched teams adopt the language (self-serve signup, a pricing page, a free tier) while every real growth lever in the business still ran through sales calls and manual onboarding. That's not product-led growth. That's a marketing site with extra steps.

What "the product leads" actually requires

Running FastRead.io taught me the honest version of the test: if you removed every human from the first session a user has with your product, would they still get to value? Not "would they understand the pitch." Would they experience the outcome, unassisted, in minutes.

For FastRead, that outcome is a finished chapter, not a tour of features. Early on, our onboarding was a guided walkthrough of settings and templates, thorough, and completely beside the point. Visitors who became paying users sat around 3%. The fix wasn't a better walkthrough. It was deleting it, and replacing it with one decision ("what's your book about?") that put a person inside a working draft in under two minutes. Paid conversion moved from 3% to 11% in a quarter.

PLG isn't a go-to-market motion you bolt onto an existing product. It's a constraint on what the product is allowed to assume about the user's patience.

The parts nobody puts in the case study

The uncomfortable part of that quarter wasn't the eventual 11%. It was the two weeks in the middle where removing the guided tour made activation numbers look worse before the new flow was tuned. Real product-led growth has a trough. If your organization isn't prepared to hold its nerve through a dip in a metric it's used to controlling with a sales call, it isn't ready for the label yet.

In practice

The one PLG change that's mattered most across every product I've run hasn't been pricing, a referral loop, or a growth hack. It's been deleting a step between signup and the user's first real "it did the thing" moment.

Three questions before you call it PLG

  • Can a stranger go from "never heard of you" to "got the outcome" with zero conversation with your team?
  • When that path breaks, does the fix start in the product, or in a script a salesperson reads better?
  • Is anyone in the room willing to watch a core metric dip for a month while the self-serve path gets good?

If the honest answer to any of those is no, the product isn't leading yet. It's being carried, politely, by people whose time doesn't show up on the same dashboard as "growth."
