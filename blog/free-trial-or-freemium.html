Free trial or freemium? What doubling paid conversion taught me

Lessons from moving a lifestyle app to freemium and subscriptions, and why onboarding mattered more than price.

Ali Ahmed Awan · July 30, 2025 · 7 min read

The question "free trial or freemium" gets debated as if it's a pricing decision. At The ENTERTAINER, moving from one-off purchases with a free trial to a freemium subscription model taught me it's mostly an onboarding decision wearing a pricing label.

Why the free trial was the wrong shape

A free trial puts a countdown on the relationship before the user has decided the product is worth one. It optimizes for "convert before the clock runs out," which pushes a team toward urgency tactics (countdown banners, exit-intent popups) instead of toward making the core value obvious fast. We had plenty of the former and not enough of the latter, and conversion sat at 13%.

What freemium actually forced us to fix

Freemium removes the clock and replaces it with a harder design problem: the free tier has to be genuinely useful, useful enough that people stick around, while still making the paid tier obviously worth it. That tension can't be solved with pricing alone. It's solved in onboarding: what the first session shows a free user, how soon they hit a limit that's meaningful instead of arbitrary, and whether upgrading feels like unlocking something they already want rather than being blocked from something they were using for free a minute ago.

Price is the easiest lever to change and almost never the one actually holding conversion back. Onboarding is harder to touch and almost always the real constraint.

We rebuilt the onboarding experience around more than 40 customer interviews, specifically hunting for the moment a free user's experience stopped feeling generous and started feeling like a wall. Move that wall too early, and you lose people before they've felt the value. Move it too late, and nobody has a reason to pay. Most of the actual work of "going freemium" was finding that line by talking to people, not by modeling it in a spreadsheet.

In practice

Paid conversion moved from 13% to 26% and customer retention from 33% to 42%, on the back of the onboarding rebuild and a Saudi Arabia launch, not a price change.

The test I'd apply before choosing either model

  • If you removed the countdown clock from your free trial today, would anyone still convert? If the honest answer is "not many," you have an onboarding problem a trial is currently masking as urgency.
  • If you gave freemium users your current free-tier limits forever, would they ever feel a reason to upgrade? If not, the free tier isn't generous, it's just restrictive on a different schedule.

Free trial versus freemium is a real decision. It's just very rarely the decision that actually moves the number. The onboarding underneath either model is.
